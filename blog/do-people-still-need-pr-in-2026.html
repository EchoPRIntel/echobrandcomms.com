Do people stillneed PR in 2026?

13 February 2026By Vitalia VasilkovaPerspective

Last year, a founder said something to me that I haven’t forgotten. We were sitting after a meeting, talking about growth, visibility, AI, all the things every company is obsessing over right now. And he looked at me and said: “Honestly, I feel like nobody knows what’s real anymore.”

Not in a dramatic way. In a tired way.

Because suddenly everything online sounds intelligent. Every company has “thought leadership”. Every startup has a polished narrative. Every founder has perfectly optimised content. AI made it possible for everyone to produce more.

But at the same time, it made trust exponentially harder to build. And I think that’s the part people are missing.

Will AI replace PR?

Everyone keeps asking. But after watching how the market is changing, I honestly think the opposite is happening. AI is making credibility more valuable than ever. For the first time in years, being believable matters more than being loud.

And you can already feel it happening everywhere. People are more sceptical. Buyers research more deeply. Investors validate faster. Journalists ignore generic stories instantly. Audiences can sense manufactured messaging within seconds.

We’re entering a market where attention is cheap, but trust is expensive.

The companies that win won’t necessarily be the companies creating the most content. They’ll be the companies people already recognise before the first conversation even starts. The companies that feel trusted. Referenced. Known.

That’s what PR actually is when it’s done well. Not headlines for ego. Not vanity metrics. Not “look how many articles we got”. Real market trust.

The numbers are staggering

  • 92% of consumers trust earned media more than advertising.
  • 80% of B2B buyers prefer articles over ads when researching companies.
  • Almost all AI-generated citations now come from non-paid sources, with the majority linked to journalistic and authoritative content.

Think about that for a second. The internet is changing from “who can publish the most?” to “who does the market trust enough to reference?” That’s an entirely different game.

One article, one partnership

Some of the strongest PR results we’ve created at Echo didn’t come from giant announcements. They came from one meaningful article. One founder interview. One story positioned correctly.

I remember one client who got featured in a publication in Asia. It wasn’t viral. It wasn’t global news. Honestly, most people would have overlooked it. But months later, someone inside one of the largest retailers in the region mentioned that exact article internally. The company suddenly felt credible. Established. Safe to take seriously.

That single piece of earned media became part of the reason the partnership moved forward. No paid ad could have created that level of trust. That’s the invisible power of PR.

What AI cannot manufacture

In the AI era, I think this effect becomes even stronger. AI can generate infinite content. Infinite opinions. Infinite noise. But it cannot manufacture real reputation. It cannot fake sustained credibility. It cannot replicate trust accumulated over time through real-world validation.

And humans still look for those signals constantly. Who’s being quoted. Who’s being referenced. Who journalists trust. Which respected publications take you seriously. Those things shape perception more than companies realise.

PR is infrastructure for authority

Which is why I believe PR is moving into a completely different role now. It’s no longer just communication. It’s infrastructure for authority.

The brands investing in credibility today are building an advantage that compounds everywhere: in AI search, in sales, in partnerships, in hiring, in investor conversations, in customer trust. And most companies are still underestimating how important that will become over the next few years.

In a world where everyone can create content, the rarest thing will be belief. That’s the future I see. And honestly, it’s a huge part of why I built Echo.

Vitalia VasilkovaFounder, Echo
